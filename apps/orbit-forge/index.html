E − e sin(E) = M
KEPLER’S EQUATION
Given orbital phase M and eccentricity e, find the angle E. The difficult corner is a stretched orbit near its closest approach to the star.
The search mutates complete bounded solver programs. Newton, Halley, damped Newton, and fixed-point steps are available building blocks. The sine/cosine approximation is supplied; its coefficients are not searched.
Evidence and limits
Every candidate runs on Gremlin’s general CUDA interpreter. GPU results are checked against an independent integer implementation. Difficult grid cases feed back into the search, which targets one tenth of the requested error budget to leave a margin.
The winner is checked on the full 256×256 grid plus 4,096 seeded validation samples using double-precision bisection as the reference. This is measured accuracy on tested inputs, not a proof over the continuous domain. Failed validation is displayed explicitly.
Inputs m and e and output E are signed 64-bit integers scaled by 2²⁸. Domain: 0 ≤ M ≤ π, 0 ≤ e ≤ 0.95. The animation mirrors the other half of the orbit.
Runtime comparisons compile actual Gremlin LLVM with Clang 18 -O3. The reference is the faster of safeguarded double-precision Newton and Halley solvers at the same requested error tolerance. Every benchmark input is separately accuracy-checked. Nine alternating trials cover two batch sizes and three input distributions. A speedup appears only if all trials win and every group’s median wins by at least 5%. It is specific to this CPU and these reference implementations, not every orbital solver.
