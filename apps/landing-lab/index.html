Each backend runs the same programs and flight scenarios. A comparison checks every returned result and instruction count.
The search chooses braking and steering expressions from a fixed grammar. The physics loop is supplied. This is controller synthesis within that grammar, not invention of a simulator from scratch.
What is measured?
Search timing includes evaluation, GPU initialization and transfers, scoring, result hashing, and progress output. Total timing also includes candidate construction and independent holdout validation. The CPU baseline is Gremlin’s single-thread interpreter, not native compiled physics or all CPU cores. Playback preserves measured timings.
Integer toy physics: up to 256 ticks, one-tick thruster delay, constant lateral wind, and 224 fuel units. Success requires reaching the pad within position and velocity limits. Holdout checks are empirical, not a safety guarantee for a real spacecraft.
